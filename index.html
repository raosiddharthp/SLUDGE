01 Client Brief

Old Street Mutual — the anchor client.

A composite of real UK retail-banking mutual patterns. Every requirement, constraint, and design decision in this document is grounded in the specific operational reality of a member-owned financial institution operating under FCA Consumer Duty, FG21/1 vulnerability guidance, and UK GDPR simultaneously — with a brand promise its current operations can't yet prove.

Old Street Mutual has spent 39 years building a brand on personal, old-fashioned fairness — "banking that remembers your name." Its actual operating capability is a five-person compliance function and a contact centre QA team that manually reviews 2% of calls. The FCA's April 2026 findings made the gap between that promise and that evidence a board-level liability, not a marketing footnote. Chairman Lord Overdraft is the named senior manager who has to attest to fair customer outcomes annually — personally, under SM&CR — on the strength of a 2% sample.

03 Problem Statement

Not "no compliance function." A compliance function that can't see 98% of what it's accountable for.

Old Street Mutual isn't failing Consumer Duty through neglect — Miranda Vetter's team works hard and genuinely cares. The failure is structural: manual QA sampling was built for an era when "spot-check and extrapolate" was an acceptable evidencing standard. The FCA's April 2026 findings closed that era. A firm reviewing 2–3% of interactions cannot show good outcomes occur consistently for everyone else — the other 97–98% isn't "probably fine," it's unmeasured, and unmeasured is no longer defensible at attestation.

Failure 1 — Disclosure evaporates
Dr. Grace Underwood tells one agent something once, and it exists nowhere the rest of the organisation can act on it. Every subsequent contact starts from zero.
Failure 2 — Nothing is disclosed at all
Percy Vere will never say he's struggling. The signal exists only in transaction and call data nobody is systematically reading across.
Failure 3 — The product creates the harm
Penny Wise isn't vulnerable in the abstract — she's failed by an app that assumes confidence she was never asked whether she has.

A fourth thread runs above all three: the trust chain terminates in nobody actually knowing. Lord Overdraft trusts Vetter's summary; Vetter's summary is built on a 2% sample she can't fully stand behind either.

Precise problem statement
Old Street Mutual has no way to evidence fair treatment across the full customer base, because its only detection mechanism — human QA sampling — structurally cannot scale past a small fraction of interactions, and structurally cannot connect a signal disclosed today to a decision made three months from now.

04 As-Is Architecture

Not one broken system. Four working systems that have never met.

Nothing in Old Street Mutual's estate is failing on its own terms — the app authenticates, the CRM logs cases, the core banking platform posts transactions correctly, the QA tooling scores calls it's given. The failure is emergent: each system does its job and stops exactly at its own boundary, and nobody owns the space between them.

SystemFunctionWhat it holds, unreachably
Member AppSelf-service banking, ~6yrs oldPenny Wise's failure pattern — uninstrumented, no drop-off telemetry
Contact Centre CRMCall logging, case notesDr. Underwood's disclosure — unstructured, unreachable outside the original case
Core Banking PlatformTransactions, direct debitsPercy Vere's signal — present, but disconnected from any interpretive logic
QA Sampling ToolBolted onto CRM, 2% weekly pullVetter's evidence — draws from CRM only, incomplete on its own terms
The finding, stated plainly
None of these four systems reads from any of the other three. A customer's transaction history, call history, and app behaviour are three separate, unconnected facts about the same person.

05 Cost of Non-Compliance

Not a hypothetical risk. A documented, recent, and repeated one — at exactly this kind of failure.

FirmFineCustomersRedressWhat failed
TSB Bank plc£10.9m (2024)232,000+£99.9mUnrealistic repayment plans, inappropriate fees
HSBC UK£6.28m (2024)1.5m£185m + £94m remediationAgents couldn't identify vulnerable customers; MI never reached governance

Total FCA fines across all firms in 2024 reached £176,045,385. Vulnerable-customer treatment sits inside this pattern, not on its margins — three separate 2024 fines related specifically to unfair treatment of customers in financial difficulty.

Read twice — the closest real mirror of §4's failure mode
HSBC's finding: inadequate discussion of customer-outcome MI at core governance committees, and insufficient call quality assurance. Almost word for word, Miranda Vetter's 2% sample and Dee Escalade's uninstrumented contact centre.

Independent base rate, not enforcement data: 26.4 million UK adults (49%) had characteristics of vulnerability as of May 2024; 13.1 million (24%) had low financial resilience. No credible basis exists for assuming Old Street Mutual's 340,000 members sit meaningfully below the national average.

Illustrative estimate for Old Street Mutual — method shown, not asserted
340,000 × 49% ≈ 166,600 members carrying a vulnerability characteristic at any time. Real per-customer exposure (fine+redress) ranges ~£127–£477. If even 1% (~1,666 members) experienced a missed outcome, low-end exposure alone: ~£210,000–£790,000 redress. Fine multiplier requires a revenue figure — established below, not fabricated here.

Revenue derivation — real ratios, one stated assumption

Leeds Building Society (real, cited): 991,000 members, £31.6bn assets → ~£31,900/member. Adjusted 10% for Old Street Mutual's 4-branch, deposit-and-everyday-banking profile vs. Leeds' national mortgage-lending scale (explicit assumption, not sourced): ~£1.08bn illustrative assets. Applying Coventry Building Society's real net interest margin (1.22%) plus fee-income allowance: ~£18 million illustrative annual relevant revenue.

Combined exposure estimate
Fine (HSBC's real 10% Level-3 multiplier, applied to £18m): ~£1.8m. Redress (above): ~£210k–£790k. Combined illustrative exposure: ~£2.0–2.6 million.

Manager Agent — runtime authority, not mechanical sequencing

Three enforced rules, each a hard gate: (1) Detection timeout or low confidence → human queue. (2) Detection output missing an evidence citation → same routing, treated identically. (3) Escalation output cannot reach HITL without a verified, complete Detection record. The grounding check is formalised as a state machine: Ungrounded → Grounded or Ungrounded → Failed-Grounding → Routed-to-Human-Queue.

Governance Agent — parallel, two jobs

Runs in parallel, not inline: (1) seven role-based dashboard lenses (ADR-04), and (2) tier translation — case-level evidence reshaped into each role's explainability type: systemic (Overdraft), methodology (Ledger), access-trail (Redact), behavioural-pattern (Trustwell), case-level (Vetter, Escalade). Generated simultaneously — explicitly not a state machine.

11 Objection Handling

Each objection stated as the persona would raise it, validated first, then answered with a specific architectural fact.

Redact — "What's the lawful basis, and how do I know this doesn't become unauthorised special-category processing?"
Valid, not resolved by intentions. Every record carries a named lawful basis and enforced retention (ADR-05), access logged per user. Doesn't solve: the pending 2026 FCA/ICO guidance hasn't landed — this design anticipates it, doesn't guarantee alignment.
Trustwell — "How do I know it's not hallucinating, and where's the human?"
Two separate answers. Hallucination: Manager Agent's grounding state-machine blocks ungrounded claims (ADR-09). Human control: nothing reaches a customer outcome without a committed HITL record (ADR-03, 08). Doesn't solve: grounding proves a claim traces to real data, not that the interpretation is always correct — which is exactly why HITL exists.
Vetter — "If this misses something the 2% sample would have caught, I signed off on it."
Legitimate. SLUDGE extends visibility from 2% to 100%; her team retains review authority. The QA tool stays running, now validated against full-coverage output. Doesn't solve: 100% flagging isn't 100% deep review — a different, more honest claim.
Ledger — "I've heard AI ROI pitches that hand-wave the number."
Correctly skeptical. §5's £2.0–2.6m figure uses two real, cited data points, every assumption stated. Doesn't solve: it's an illustrative estimate for a fictional company, labelled as such throughout.
Escalade — "Three vendor rollouts made my team's job harder, not easier."
Earned. Single combined HITL screen (ADR-03), evidence checkable in seconds. Doesn't solve: rollout fatigue is a change-management problem as much as a technical one — addressed in §13's phasing, not the architecture alone.
